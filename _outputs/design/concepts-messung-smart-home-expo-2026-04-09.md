```
================================================================
CONCEPT 1 — THE CONTROL ROOM
Direction: Recreate the visual language of an industrial SCADA control room — 
           the stand IS the interface.
```

**Spatial moves:**
- **Primary:** Back wall (4m wide) treated as a live control panel — graphical grid of indicator lights, status displays, and product mounts at eye height. Products emerge from the panel, not placed in front of it.
- **Secondary:** PLC demo station at front-left — custom-built console housing at waist height with product live and operable. Visitor activates it; something visible changes on the back wall (light, graphic, status indicator).
- **Tertiary:** Matte dark-grey laminate on all walls with white/orange/green accent lighting (traffic light / status colour coding). Two stools at front-right for informal conversation — no table, lean-in posture.

**Visitor journey:**
1. Entry (4m open front): back wall visible from aisle — grid of lights reads as a live system, not a display
2. Hook: demo PLC at front draws visitors who've seen PLCs before — immediate recognition
3. Engage: visitor activates demo, back wall responds — memorable, tactile
4. Meeting: stools at front-right, away from the demo zone — low-pressure conversation area
5. Exit: one clear brand message at top of back wall: "Connected plant intelligence, made in India"

**Must-have checklist:** Demo PLC ✅ | Product wall ✅ | Meeting stools ✅ | Storage ❓ | AV ❓
**Rough CP band:** ₹2.4L–₹3.0L
**Margin at mid-range (₹2.7L):** 57.1% ✅ PASS
**Why this works:** Messung's buyers are engineers and plant managers. A control-room aesthetic creates instant professional recognition — they know exactly what they're looking at and why it matters. No explanation needed.
**Risk:** If the back wall graphic isn't executed with precision, it reads as cheap printed signage rather than a system. Needs high-resolution print or digital skin — not vinyl stickers.

---

```
================================================================
CONCEPT 2 — THE SIGNAL
Direction: Flip the industrial convention — show nothing but the intelligence. 
           Dark, minimal, luminous. Messung as a premium product, not a factory tool.
```

**Spatial moves:**
- **Primary:** Full back wall in near-black (charcoal / graphite panel). Single horizontal LED light bar at 1.5m height (eye level), running the full 4m width — warm white, diffused. Products mounted above and below the light bar, each on individual floating brackets, lit from behind. No shelving visible. Products appear to float.
- **Secondary:** PLC demo at centre-front on a low black plinth (350mm raised), backlit from below in cool white. Clean acrylic or glass top surface. Product is a specimen — handled only on request. Creates deliberate stopping power.
- **Tertiary:** Typography: single brand message in reverse (white text on dark): "The plant is thinking." Two matte-black stools, low profile, designer — to the left. No other furniture.

**Visitor journey:**
1. Entry: dark, glowing stand visible from 10m — contrast against typical white/colourful neighbours draws attention
2. Hook: floating products and the lone PLC specimen create "what is this?" curiosity — even from non-technical visitors
3. Engage: Messung rep walks the product wall, explains connectivity — the dark backdrop makes the demonstration feel precise
4. Meeting: stools away from the hero plinth — the visitor sits because they want to, not because they're corralled
5. Exit: "The plant is thinking." lingers — product promise, not product name

**Must-have checklist:** Demo PLC ✅ | Product wall ✅ | Meeting stools ✅ | Storage ❌ (no visible storage — must be hidden behind back wall panel) | AV ❓ (optional — a subtle looping animation on a small monitor could work, but not required)
**Rough CP band:** ₹2.8L–₹3.5L (LED light bar + premium plinth + floating brackets add cost)
**Margin at mid-range (₹3.15L):** 50.0% ✅ PASS
**Why this works:** Smart Home Expo has consumer brands everywhere — colourful, warm, domestic. Messung arrives looking like none of them. The contrast IS the message: this is industrial precision meeting smart design. Exactly what "Smart industrial control for the connected plant" means as a brand posture.
**Risk:** Dark stands can feel uninviting if the lighting isn't warm enough. The LED bar temperature and diffusion are critical. Mangesh to specify this precisely in the fabrication brief.

---

```
================================================================
CONCEPT 3 — THE CONNECTED LINE
Direction: Challenge the "product wall + demo table" assumption entirely — 
           make the connection the hero, not the product.
```

**Spatial moves:**
- **Primary:** A single horizontal "network line" runs the full width of the stand at 1.6m height — a physical element (powder-coated aluminium rail or stretched acrylic) connecting every product on the wall. Products are mounted on/along this line, with short cables or connectors visible between them. The line continues past the edge panels slightly — visually suggesting it extends beyond the stand. One word below: CONNECTED.
- **Secondary:** PLC demo is mounted directly ON the line, not on a separate table. It is part of the system — not separate from it. Interactive: Messung rep can toggle a physical switch that lights up individual nodes on the line, demonstrating what "connected" means in real plant terms.
- **Tertiary:** White walls (break from dark to create contrast in a potentially dark hall). The line in Messung's brand colour (confirm with Gitesh — likely blue or orange). Meeting stools at front corner — no spatial separation from the demo, more collaborative posture.

**Visitor journey:**
1. Entry: the physical line is immediately legible as a "system" concept from the aisle — unlike any other product wall
2. Hook: the toggle/activation moment on the PLC draws engineers by instinct
3. Engage: "Here's how your plant gets connected — from the sensor to the SCADA" — the line becomes a storytelling device
4. Meeting: stools right next to the demo — co-discovery mode, not presentation mode
5. Exit: visitor has physically touched the connected system — highest memory retention of all three concepts

**Must-have checklist:** Demo PLC ✅ | Product wall ✅ | Meeting stools ✅ | Storage ❓ | AV ❓ (the toggle/node system could be considered interactive AV without a screen)
**Rough CP band:** ₹2.5L–₹3.2L (aluminium rail system is cost-efficient; switch/toggle adds ₹15–25k)
**Margin at mid-range (₹2.85L):** 54.8% ✅ PASS
**Why this works:** The line is a tactile, physical representation of the product promise. Most competitors show products. First Rain makes visitors feel the connection. At a Smart Home show where the entire pitch is "everything connects," owning the metaphor physically is the strongest possible move.
**Risk:** The toggle/node interaction must work 100% of the time. If it glitches on Day 1, the hero moment becomes a liability. Fabrication must be tested end-to-end before delivery.
